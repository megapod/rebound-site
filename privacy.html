Privacy Policy — Rebound

App: Rebound (package com.yehuda.rebound) · Developer: Yehuda P. · Contact: yehuda.g.p@gmail.com · Last updated: November 10, 2026

Overview

Rebound is a basketball game you can play alone or online. This policy explains what data is processed when you play, and by whom. The short version: you can play without any account, and the game never asks for your e-mail or phone number. Google services power sign-in, ads and usage statistics. The game has no in-app purchases. Our own game server runs online play while you play, and receives anonymous statistics about how the game is played. We never sell data.

Data stored on your device only

Settings, progress, credits and the time of the daily reminder are saved on your device. They are deleted when you uninstall the game.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The game shows ads served by Google AdMob. To serve and measure ads, Google may collect and process data such as your device's advertising ID, IP address, approximate location derived from IP, and ad-interaction events. This processing is governed by Google's own policies:

  • Google Privacy Policy
  • How Google uses information from ads

Where required by law (for example in the EEA/UK), you will be shown a consent form (Google User Messaging Platform) before personalized ads are served, and you may choose non-personalized ads. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in your device's Google settings (Settings → Google → Ads).

Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

The game uses Google Analytics for Firebase to understand how the game is used in general: how many people open it, how often they come back, and how long a session lasts. For this purpose Google collects and processes data such as an app-instance identifier, your device's advertising ID, device model and operating-system version, country derived from IP address, and usage events (for example, first open and session start). The game does not send your name, email address, or anything you type.

Sign-in (Google Play Games and Firebase Authentication)

When the game starts, it signs you in to Google Play Games silently, if your phone is signed in to it. For online play the game exchanges that sign-in for a Firebase Authentication token (by Google), and our server checks the token so nobody can play online as someone else. Through them we receive your Play Games player name and an account identifier. Google keeps a sign-in record for us in Firebase Authentication. See Google's privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

Online play

Our game server (a "relay", run by us on our own computer and on rented machines at Oracle Cloud and Google Cloud) finds you an opponent (from version 1.10.16 (50), when no opponent is found, you may be matched with a computer-controlled opponent), opens rooms by code, and passes the moves between the two players. The moves and the state of a game are held only in memory while it is open. The server sees your IP address while you are connected, to deliver the game's messages and to limit abuse. To recognize our own test devices and to see which of the countries we focus on players come from, the server also checks your IP address against lists of address ranges for a few countries when you connect, and notes only which country matched, if any, together with the random number described under "Anonymous game statistics". The IP address itself is not stored. From version 1.9.3 (27) the game's messages to the server, your sign-in token included, are encrypted on your phone so that only our server can read them; older versions of the game still send them readable until they are updated. The server's logs, which can include the IP address and the account identifier of a connection, are kept for up to two weeks and then deleted.

Playing on the same Wi-Fi

Two phones on the same Wi-Fi network can play each other directly. The game finds the other phone on that network; these messages stay on the local network and do not reach our server.

Anonymous game statistics

The game makes a random number on your phone. It is not your name, your account, your advertising ID or your phone's serial number, and a new one is made if you reinstall the game. While the game is open, it tells our server that it is here (with that number, the game's version, and which screen and mode are open) and receives the game's settings, including which test group your phone belongs to. Once a day (with the day the game was first opened), and when certain things happen in the game (a credit is earned or used, a game starts or ends and its score, a rewarded ad is offered, loaded, shown or failed, a step of the tutorial, the daily reminder, the daily court, a search for an opponent was opened or left, a court was left or stayed at, whether sign-in succeeded, whether a newer version was found, the first time a game mode is opened, from version 1.10.16 (50) when you were matched with the computer because no opponent was found and after how many seconds, and whether the sign-in offer was shown and what you chose, from version 1.10.8 (42) how many shots went in when a shooting round ends, and when a card for our other games is shown or tapped and which game it was for; and, from version 1.9.4 (28), how the previous run ended if the game crashed, froze or was closed by the system: whether it crashed, stopped responding or was closed for lack of memory, whether the game was on the screen, the last two game events, the screen and mode that were open, and how many seconds the game had been running; from version 1.10.16 (50), in a separate note sent only after such a run, your phone's model and how much memory it has), the game sends a short note with that number and a few short details (for example the game mode, the score, the number of credits, or why an ad did not load). If your phone is one of Google's automated test devices, the note says so. These messages are encrypted on your phone so that only our server can read them. We use them to see how the game is played, and we cannot tie them to you. They are kept for up to 90 days and then deleted.

Daily reminder

If you allow notifications, the game sets one reminder a day on your phone. It is made by your phone, not sent from our server.

Our other games

From version 1.10.8 (42), the game shows a card for our other games, and some of the clips you watch to play promote one of our other games instead of showing an ad from Google AdMob; no ad is requested from Google for those clips. You get the same reward as for an ad when you watch to the end; installing or tapping is never required. The game may check on your phone whether our other game is already installed, so as not to promote it to you; that result stays on your phone and is never sent. If you are an adult player, the store link carries a short label saying that you came from this game, and, if "Usage statistics" is on, the game records in Google Analytics that the card was shown or tapped. In protected mode the link has no label and nothing is recorded in Analytics. Our server receives that a card was shown or tapped, as one of the notes described under "Anonymous game statistics". From version 1.10.11 (45), if you are an adult player and installed this game from such a card in another of our games, the game reads from Google Play, the first time you open it, which of our games the link came from, and tells our server only that (for example "came from Sudoku Race"), once, as one of those notes. Nothing else from Google Play's install information is kept or sent.

Files downloaded from our site

The game downloads two small files from our site, which is hosted on GitHub Pages: the list of our game servers, and a file that says whether a newer version exists. As with any website, the host receives your IP address. No other information about you is sent.

Your age and protected mode

From version 1.10.5 (39), the first time you open the game it asks which country you are in (filled in from your phone's SIM card, mobile network, time zone or language, and you can change it) and whether you are 18 or over. You must answer before you play. From version 1.10.8 (42), where Google Play provides an age signal for your account, the game first asks Google Play for it, without showing anything: Google returns only an age range. If the range is under 18 the game uses protected mode, if it is 18 or over the game uses adult mode, and otherwise it asks you the question. The range is used once, on your phone, and is never stored or sent anywhere. Until you answer, the game turns on no usage statistics and asks for no ads.

If you are 18 or over, the game works as described above, and in Settings you can turn off usage statistics and personalized ads at any time.

If you are under 18, the game runs in protected mode: Google Analytics for Firebase collects nothing, Google AdMob is told that you are under the age of consent and shows only non-personalized ads suitable for teens, and the switches in Settings stay off. Online play stays available without any account: in protected mode the game never asks Google for your Play Games player name or for a Firebase Authentication account, and our server knows you only by the random number described in "Anonymous game statistics". Google Play Games on your phone may still sign in to Google on its own when the game starts, as it does for any game that uses it; nothing from that sign-in reaches us in protected mode.

Your answer (adult or protected mode), the country and how it was set (the question, Settings, or an earlier question in the game) are sent only to our server, with the random number described in "Anonymous game statistics", and are kept for up to 90 days like the rest of those statistics. They are never sent to Google Analytics. The game does not ask for your date of birth or your exact age.

Children and teenagers

Rebound is not directed at children under 13. We do not ask for anyone's name, e-mail or phone number. If you are a parent and believe your child has used the game's online features, write to yehuda.g.p@gmail.com and we will delete what our server holds for that player.

Data sharing and selling

We do not sell personal data. We share data only with the Google services and the site hosting described above, which receive the data listed in their sections and operate under their own policies.

Data retention and deletion

Our server keeps no player profile for Rebound: no names, friends or tokens are stored. It keeps only which test group each random number belongs to, so that the same phone stays in the same group, for up to 90 days. Anonymous statistics are kept for up to 90 days. The sign-in record at Firebase Authentication is deleted after 90 days without a sign-in, or earlier on request. To request deletion, see Delete your account, or send an e-mail to yehuda.g.p@gmail.com with the subject "Rebound - delete my data" and your Play Games player name. We delete within 30 days and confirm by e-mail. Usage statistics are held by Google Analytics, in aggregated or pseudonymous form, for a limited period set by that service. Local data is deleted when you uninstall the game.

Your rights and choices

You can ask us for a copy of the data our server holds about you, ask us to correct it or to delete it, and name someone to act for you if you die or become unable to. Write to yehuda.g.p@gmail.com; we answer within 30 days. You can withdraw a consent you gave at any time, as easily as you gave it: from version 1.10.5 (39), "Usage statistics" and "Personalised ads" can be turned off in the game's Settings, and "My data" there opens an e-mail to us with your random number; any other consent can be withdrawn by writing to the same address.

Questions and complaints go to the developer, Yehuda P., at the same address. If you are in India, you may also complain to the Data Protection Board of India. This policy is written in English; write to us if you need it in another language.

Changes to this policy

When the game starts processing new data, this page is updated before that version is released, and the "last updated" date changes.
